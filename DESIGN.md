# Design System: Lake Cinciș

## Overview

**Creative North Star: "The Lake Survey Desk"**

This is a provisional HUD for a real-time asset feasibility test. The lake and the playable world stay visible and carry the experience; the interface sits at the margins like a compact field desk for changing camera, light, material comparison, source coverage, and rendering diagnostics. It is designed to help the team inspect a real scene and describe what has actually been assembled.

The visual language is cool, quiet, and geometric so it can sit over water, roads, forest, and cabin assets without pretending that the art direction is finished. Barlow supplies readable utility text while Barlow Condensed gives location and panel headings a compact survey-label voice. The rejected serif, cream, and green HUD is not part of this system. The richer playful 3D target remains under evaluation; this contract records the implemented feasibility HUD only.

The current local settlement pass gives that HUD a stable inspection subject: 30 seeded cabins along the southern road-facing shore, split between uphill sites and a band between the road and reserved lakefront. Six complete whole-house designs are distributed five each by a stable Fisher–Yates world shuffle: Timber veranda, Forest A-frame, Sage porch, Blue wraparound, Copper shelter, and Ivory pergola. The runtime library is documented in `apps/web/public/assets/cabin-library.json`, with the original reference sheet at `docs/art-direction/cabin-reference-grid.png`, the three variant references at `docs/art-direction/cabin-variants-grid.png` generated by the built-in image_gen tool from `docs/art-direction/cabin-variants-prompt.txt`, and actual Blender renders at `docs/art-direction/cabins-blender-final.png` and `docs/art-direction/cabin-variants-blender.png`. The editable source is `assets/blender/Reference_Cabin_Library.blend`; only this library is active there and prior scene workspaces are retained as backups. All number plates use validated author-defined post/wall mounts above 1.5m with raycast geometry gaps below 5cm. A shared lakefront fire circle and local E-key fishing activity establish small social and activity cues. The prior 16-family feasibility report remains historical and does not describe this six-design library. These are local prototype facts; they do not establish network persistence, cabin ownership, pixel-perfect image reproduction, or final art approval.

**Key Characteristics:**

- Scene-first composition with small, peripheral HUD surfaces.
- Cool paper, slate ink, muted blue accent, and thin rules.
- Near-square controls with underlined active camera navigation.
- Explicit imported, built, mixed, map-data, and untested provenance.
- Provisional labels for coverage and rendering measurements.
- Six fixed, complete cabin designs distributed five each by a stable world shuffle.
- Local home and north-up geography navigation with restrained map landmarks.

## Colors

The palette is a cool neutral field that keeps the world legible. Slate blue marks selected detail and map context; muted brown, violet, teal, and grey distinguish inventory provenance without becoming a decorative palette.

### Primary

- **Slate blue accent** (#5f7f94): Selected camera detail, quiet links, and small location or world signals.
- **Focus blue** (#688ca4): Keyboard focus and selection feedback.

### Secondary

- **Imported slate** (#5e7d91): Imported environment families in the inventory.
- **Built umber** (#8a7864): Authored or custom-built scene families.
- **Mixed violet** (#7e648d): Assemblies combining authored and imported parts.
- **Map-data teal** (#2f7181): Geographic source data such as roads.
- **Untested grey** (#aab5bd): Families still pending the asset test.

### Neutral

- **Cool paper** (#f0f3f5): HUD plaques, camera navigation, panels, and the footer.
- **Deep slate ink** (#24313b): Headings, primary actions, strong rules, and readable foreground text.
- **Muted slate** (#566672): Supporting copy, controls hints, and provenance detail.
- **Lake air** (#cbdde5): The browser canvas fallback and the open world field behind the scene.
- **Quiet rule** (#ccd4db): Panel dividers and inventory row rules.
- **Hover surface** (#dbe4ea): Small control hover and selected icon backgrounds.
- **Panel underlay** (#e4eaef): Inventory panel footer and secondary tonal layering.
- **White** (#ffffff): Primary action text and selected dark control text.

### Named Rules

**The Cool-Field Rule.** Keep HUD surfaces cool and neutral so the lake, forest, and imported materials remain the visual subject. Accent and provenance colors are signals, not decoration.

## Typography

**Display Font:** Barlow Condensed (with sans-serif fallback)

**Body Font:** Barlow (with sans-serif fallback)

**Label/Mono Font:** Barlow for compact labels and tabular rendering values.

**Character:** Barlow is plain, sturdy, and readable in small controls. Barlow Condensed gives the location plaque, panel titles, and loading state a compact field-note character without introducing a separate editorial voice.

### Hierarchy

- **Display** (600, 42px, line-height 1): Loading and recovery titles; uppercase and compact.
- **Headline** (600, 32px, line-height 1.05): Scene notes and prominent feasibility panel titles.
- **Title** (600, 28px, line-height 1): Location plaque and inventory heading.
- **Body** (400, 12px, line-height 1.8): Scene notes, inventory detail, and explanatory copy.
- **Label** (600, 10px, line-height 1.2, 0.12em tracking, uppercase where used): Location labels, test badges, small status readouts, and compact UI metadata.

### Named Rules

**The Utility Pairing Rule.** Use Barlow Condensed for location and panel headings, Barlow for everything users need to read, and do not reintroduce a serif display voice into the HUD.

## Layout

The game shell is a full-viewport canvas with an absolute HUD layer. The world stays beneath the controls; plaques, buttons, and panels use opaque cool-paper surfaces so their text remains readable without blur. The desktop top bar sits 24px from the top and 28px from the sides. The location plaque anchors left, while the world-test actions anchor right. The light, avatar, and texture tools form a narrow vertical stack near the right edge at roughly 42% viewport height.

Camera navigation is centered near the bottom at 58px, with a compact control hint below it. Survey view, Cabins, Home, On foot, and the Character inspection view share one underlined navigation row. Walking exposes W/A/S/D, Shift, Space, and drag-to-look hints; survey, Cabins, Home, and Character expose orbit and zoom hints. In Cabins mode, keys 1 through 6 inspect the six complete designs; the active model is selected as a coherent whole house, without independent roof or fixture distortion. Home mode focuses locally selected plot index 18. The lower left carries the south-shore readout when the mode permits it, while the lower right carries the feasibility coverage readout. A full-width footer keeps the local-prototype/elevation-proxy note, map attribution, and live renderer metrics visible.

The inventory panel is a right-side surface with a 420px maximum width, 110px top offset, and 50px bottom offset on desktop. At 1100px and below, lower readouts move upward to clear the footer. At 650px and below, the top bar, controls, coverage readout, footer, and inventory panel compress to the 14–17px side rhythm, with the panel occupying the available width. The spacing rhythm is 4, 8, 12, 16, 20, and 28px.

The settlement layout is deliberately stable for inspection: 15 cabins sit uphill of the southern road and 15 sit between the road and a reserved lakefront band. Six fixed whole-house designs are distributed five each by a stable Fisher–Yates world shuffle: Timber veranda, Forest A-frame, Sage porch, Blue wraparound, Copper shelter, and Ivory pergola. Cabin number plates use author-defined post/wall mounts above 1.5m with raycast geometry gaps below 5cm. The shared fire circle reads as common lakefront ground rather than private property. A compact north-up minimap uses the real lake and roads, cabin markers, the selected home marker, fishing landmarks, and the shared campfire landmark; M expands it and H or the Home button focuses the local home camera. Desktop keyboard play is the current scope; mobile optimization and testing are deferred.

## Elevation & Depth

The HUD uses flat tonal layering instead of shadows. Depth comes from the live 3D world, light and material changes, opaque paper surfaces, thin borders, and occasional 2px ink rules along the edge of a plaque or action group. The inventory panel enters with a short 0.18s translation/fade; other state changes use short transitions. The system does not use decorative blur, glass, or ambient card shadows.

### Named Rules

**The Flat HUD Rule.** Keep resting controls flat and legible. Use tonal separation and rules for structure, reserving motion for a state change and leaving the world responsible for depth.

## Shapes

Controls are near-square, with 0px, 1px, or 2px radii. Icon buttons use a 2px radius; tool controls use a 1px radius; camera navigation and plaques remain square. Active camera navigation is an underline, not a filled pill. Inventory status markers are small square dots, and row separators are 1px rules. Focus uses a visible 2px outline with a 4px offset. There are no decorative rounded cards or pill-shaped tags in the implemented HUD, even where a coverage control uses a `coverage-pill` class name.

## Components

The component language is a small field kit: opaque paper surfaces, strong text hierarchy, and state expressed through rules, underlines, and restrained tonal shifts.

### Buttons

- **Icon actions:** Near-square 40×40px transparent controls in the paper action group. They use Phosphor icons, gain the hover surface on hover, and expose a 2px focus outline.
- **Primary:** The scene-notes CTA uses deep slate ink, white text, 13px 18px padding, and a 2px radius. Hover shifts to a darker blue slate; it includes an inline Footprints icon.
- **Secondary / text:** Scene notes and metric export actions use transparent backgrounds, Barlow labels, and underlined text with a small directional or download icon.

### Navigation

- **Camera row:** A paper navigation strip contains Survey view, Cabins, Home, On foot, and Character. Each item is Barlow 600 at 12px with a Phosphor icon; the active item uses deep slate text and a 2px bottom underline. Hover shifts the text toward the accent blue.
- **Keyboard hint:** Walking uses compact white keycaps with a quiet bottom rule. Orbit, zoom, and pan copy is plain text with centered separators.

### Cards / Containers

- **Location plaque:** Cool paper, 13px/18px internal padding, a 2px ink bottom rule, a duotone Phosphor map icon, and a Barlow Condensed uppercase title. It identifies Lake Cinciș, the south shore, and the world study without a marketing headline.
- **Inventory panel:** A bordered paper surface with a heading row, provenance legend, scrollable rows, and a slightly darker footer. Rows use 1px quiet rules, status icons, source detail, and a small provenance tag.
- **Scene-notes panel:** A centered paper panel with a 3px ink top rule, compact close action, explanatory copy, and the primary CTA.

### Readouts

- **Coverage:** The feasibility coverage control pairs a large tabular percentage with `integrated of 20 asset families`, a smaller label, and a Phosphor chevron. It opens the inventory; it reports checklist coverage, not game completion.
- **Live renderer metrics:** The footer output reports settled rolling-window FPS, triangles, draw calls, and avatar count. It is diagnostic evidence for this test, not multiplayer capacity certification.

### Settlement and Activities

- **Cabin library:** The local scene renders 30 stable cabin placements, 15 uphill of the southern road and 15 between the road and reserved lakefront. Six whole-house designs are distributed five each by the stable Fisher–Yates shuffle in `apps/web/src/cabin-designs.ts`: Timber veranda (`Lake_Cabin_Gable`), Forest A-frame (`Lake_Cabin_AFrame`), Sage porch (`Lake_Cabin_Sage`), Blue wraparound (`Lake_Cabin_GableBlue`), Copper shelter (`Lake_Cabin_AFrameCopper`), and Ivory pergola (`Lake_Cabin_SagePergola`). The first three follow `docs/art-direction/cabin-reference-grid.png`; the additional three follow `docs/art-direction/cabin-variants-grid.png`, generated with the built-in image_gen tool from `docs/art-direction/cabin-variants-prompt.txt`, and rendered in `docs/art-direction/cabin-variants-blender.png`. The editable library is `assets/blender/Reference_Cabin_Library.blend`; these are faithful reconstruction targets without a pixel-perfect or final-art claim.
- **Number plates:** Author-defined post/wall mounts sit above 1.5m and have validated raycast geometry gaps below 5cm. Their stable cabin numbers support inspection and navigation; they do not indicate ownership.
- **Shared fire circle:** A single lakefront fire circle is a common social landmark. Its nearby E prompt uses the existing `--paper`, `--ink`, and `--line` palette with the same compact Barlow treatment as the rest of the HUD.
- **Fishing prompt:** At local fishing spots, E casts, waits for a bite, and reels. Catch records are saved only in this browser, and the prompt says so; it does not represent network persistence or shared multiplayer state.

### Home and Map Navigation

- **Local home:** `HomeMarker.tsx` presents a visible “Your cabin” label and porch light for the locally selected plot. `home.ts` stores home index 18 in browser storage and maps the home against the real geography; it is a local preview choice that a server-backed room can replace.
- **North-up minimap:** `Minimap.tsx` uses the real lake and road geometry with Barlow/Phosphor/slate UI and geography colors for land, water, roads, cabins, fishing spots, and the shared campfire. The player arrow rotates against a fixed north-up map, while the selected home is highlighted and remains bounded when off-screen. M expands the map; H or the Home button focuses the selected cabin.
- **Access boundary:** Visits require the owner's approval, while administrator entry is independent of owner approval; both rules are separate from the local home choice. Interiors, entry, invitations, and administrator authentication are not implemented in this preview and remain the next server-backed step.

### Icons

Use Phosphor icons at the sizes present in the implementation, generally 15–29px. The location map uses the duotone weight; controls use regular weight. Icons sit beside text when they explain a mode or action and do not replace accessible labels. Any standalone documentation snippet should inline its SVG path data so it remains portable.

## Do's and Don'ts

### Do:

- **Do** let the real lake, roads, forest, water, and assembled assets lead the viewport.
- **Do** keep the cool-paper/slate palette and Barlow/Barlow Condensed pairing tied to the extracted implementation.
- **Do** use underlined active camera navigation for Survey view, Cabins, Home, On foot, and Character inspection.
- **Do** expose inventory provenance, feasibility coverage, and live renderer diagnostics in plain language.
- **Do** label the 30-avatar control as a rendering test and keep the asset direction explicitly provisional.
- **Do** keep each cabin plot on one coherent whole-house design and use Cabins mode keys 1 through 6 to inspect the library.
- **Do** keep seeded cabin placement, the shared fire circle, and browser-only catch persistence described as local prototype behavior.
- **Do** treat the imagegen reference grids as architectural references and the Blender renders/library as the current reconstruction evidence, without claiming pixel-perfect matching.
- **Do** keep the local home marker and north-up minimap clearly separate from server ownership, interiors, entry, invitations, and administrator authentication.
- **Do** point to the final verified home, minimap, cabin-number, and cabin-variant captures under `docs/feasibility/captures/` when showing the current preview.
- **Do** preserve keyboard focus, reduced-motion behavior, and map attribution at every supported viewport.

### Don't:

- **Don't** restore the rejected generic serif, cream, and green HUD.
- **Don't** replace the near-square controls and underlines with decorative pills, blur, or generic shadow cards.
- **Don't** cover the lake with a marketing headline or imply that the current scene is final art approval.
- **Don't** describe asset-family coverage as multiplayer completion, game completion, or a performance certification.
- **Don't** imply that a seeded cabin is owned, that a catch is network-persistent, or that local activity state is shared between players.
- **Don't** reintroduce procedural roof deformation, mixed parts, or random proportions that break a complete whole-house design.
- **Don't** call the reference grids or Blender libraries pixel-perfect reproductions or final art approval.
- **Don't** invent unimplemented game interactions, final outfits, terrain elevation, shoreline treatment, or approved 3D art direction.
