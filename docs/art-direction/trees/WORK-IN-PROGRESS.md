# Cinciș trees: prototype and asset audit

Status: **oak v2 restored to the draft branch for further testing after the user said they like the oak**. The user wants other species to share its finish while retaining their distinct identities. The oak still looks dark in game and the distant transition is not approved; the draft is not ready to merge. The shared preview and production are unchanged. Studio renders are distinct from gameplay captures; generated images are references/source art, not 3D models.

## Regional direction

The [Hunedoara municipality's 2017 report](https://www.primariahunedoara.ro/files/pages_files/Raport_de_activitate_anul_2017.pdf), page 8, describes sessile oak (*Quercus petraea*, gorun) across hills from Teliuc toward Ghelari and oak–beech forest in higher parts of the area. The report concerns the wider region rather than an individual survey of the lake's shoreline. Use gorun as the first family, then consider beech for higher/more shaded hills. The [municipal annex](https://emol.ro/pluginfile.php/1/local_emol/documentanexa/111/HCL-157-2021-Anexe-Statut.pdf) describes hornbeam and shrubs including hazel and hawthorn in the municipality; exact lake-edge placement needs stronger evidence. Do not claim a shoreline inventory or assume that every present pine is native to a particular spot.

## Source and reproducibility

- `cincis-oak-concept.png`: generated reference; input was `docs/animations/captures/performance-survey-desktop.png` from the legacy workspace as a style reference. The requested result was one stylized oak in full three-quarter and side views with foliage detail, no scenery, text or props. The game screenshot depicts an earlier world build, not the current runtime.
- `oak-foliage-generated.png`: generated transparent foliage spray, used as an original image texture; see `imagegen-prompts.txt` for the exact prompts.
- `scripts/build-cincis-oak.mjs` is the editable, deterministic source; run it from the project root with Node 24 after `npm ci`. It exports `cincis-oak-prototype.glb` here. A processed 512×512 PNG is embedded in the GLB; the original remains alongside this document.
- `oak-prototype-render.png` and the front, side, and overhead PNGs are studio renders of the current card prototype; they are not game screenshots. The side and overhead angles reveal thin card edges and unnatural branching; these defects prevent acceptance.
- Current prototype: 816 triangles, two mesh/material groups, 512×512 masked PNG and 397,096-byte GLB. This is only a geometry/size comparison, **not** a frame-rate improvement. Masked cards, their overlap, shadows and reflections still cost GPU work. The current production `CommonTree_1` catalog reports 6,265 triangles and 304,208 bytes; the card prototype's PNG is larger even though its geometry is smaller.
- One failed prototype used solid faceted foliage blobs and did not resemble the reference. The card version revealed a black-padding bug during image resize; setting transparent padding fixed the rendered rectangles. Transparent borders must be checked in the export, not inferred from the source.

## Acceptance before integration

Improve trunk transitions and bark, assess repeated cards and silhouette from several camera angles, and compare the model against existing trees in the real game lighting and camera. Create an impostor from the approved actual model and verify scale, grounding, LOD transitions, trunk collision, culling, and shadow behavior. Compare matched viewport, camera, density, render passes and hardware; record frame timing and draw calls rather than inferring speed from file size. Keep the shared preview undisturbed until the replacement is ready and integration is coordinated.

## Inventory and priorities

This is a source/catalog triage, not a complete visual or performance audit. Counts below are catalog values, not measurements of every instance in the current frame.

1. **Trees:** `CommonTree_1` (6,265 triangles), `CommonTree_3` (3,505), `Pine_2` (3,648), `Pine_4` (3,370); four source models, shared instancing and baked crossed-card distant impostors. Prioritize locally grounded broadleaf silhouette and matched LOD; retain existing assets until validated.
2. **Understory:** bush (900 triangles), fern (288), flower group (755), grass (155). Compare silhouette and density in actual play; use local hazel/hawthorn and meadow references, and measure total instances, shadow/alpha cost before changing them.
3. **Rocks and shoreline:** two primary rock models (342/244 triangles) plus pebble, terrain, water and road. Check repetition and blending against Cinciș shore references before replacing low-cost geometry.
4. **Buildings and props:** original cabin variants and interiors, plus catalog fence, door, windows, bench, table, lantern, barrel and crate. Evaluate at game scale and coordinate with the chats owning cabins and seating. A catalog count alone does not establish an art defect or a runtime bottleneck.
5. **Characters and vehicles:** custom lakeside character, animation library, scooter and planes are separate systems with existing owners. Preserve the rig, collision, controls and multiplayer behavior; review separately in motion.
6. **Fishing, last:** `character-actions.ts` constructs a simple cylinder rod, sphere/cone fish and procedural fishing line, and contains bite/reel gesture clips. Prioritize identifiable rod/reel, a locally appropriate fish silhouette, hand grip and cast/reel/catch motion once the tree workflow is proven. Verify the interaction and remote animation rather than just a static mesh.

## Second iteration, 29 October 2026

`cincis-oak-v2.glb` is a second prototype built by `scripts/build-cincis-oak-v2.mjs`. It uses smoothly tapered curved trunks and branches, bent foliage cards, and `oak-bark-generated.png` as an original albedo source. Front, side and overhead studio captures are in the corresponding `oak-v2-*.png` files. It is more organic than v1 but the canopy still repeats visibly and has spiky card edges. It was temporarily integrated for validation and subsequently removed from the runtime branch after the quality reassessment below.

The export has 2,304 triangles, two mesh/material groups, a 512×512 masked foliage PNG and a 256×256 bark JPEG, and is 197,976 bytes. It is byte-identical across two rebuilds on the same toolchain. Palette encoding reduced the foliage PNG from 345,814 bytes to 91,727 bytes without changing its 512×512 GPU dimensions. Of 262,144 pixels, 57 changed sides of the 0.38 mask threshold compared to the unquantized resized source; a studio render was inspected. This saves transfer bytes, not necessarily GPU memory or frame time. Test coverage checks the actual bark winding and foliage export properties, including a regression for an observed inverted-winding defect.

`oak-v2-impostor-experimental.png` is an orthographic RGBA bake from the v2 model, not the concept image. At 256×384 it uses world dimensions 7.6294384 × 11.4441576, with the model bottom aligned to the quad bottom. This unusually tall frame preserves the broad crown with the existing impostor aspect ratio, leaving transparent space above the tree; using the dimensions instead of the model height for collision would overstate trunk height. The bitmap is an experiment and has not been checked against the game's current distant tint, shadow/LOD transition, or collision. `scripts/bake-cincis-oak-impostor.mjs` can regenerate it without a server; it requires Playwright and a compatible Chromium, configured through `PLAYWRIGHT_MODULE` and `CHROME_PATH` if not installed in the project. These dependencies are not bundled as runtime game dependencies.

### Fixed-camera browser check

A local, built worktree was loaded with browser request interception: only `CommonTree_1.glb` was substituted by v2, while all other files came from the same build. External requests and WebSockets were blocked, no listener or server was started, and the shared preview was untouched. `oak-v2-game-substitution.png` and `oak-original-game-comparison.png` capture the built-in performance lab's north view with the same offline setup. The model request confirms the 197,976-byte v2 file was consumed. This is not an integrated runtime change: `CommonTree_1.png` remained the original distant impostor, so the replacement would pop at its LOD switch and is not ready to ship.

Both brief runs used headless Chrome SwiftShader, DPR 1.5, an 800×600 browser viewport and the same camera; they captured 14 and 12 frames respectively, including loading stalls and giant long tasks. Renderer averages and frame timings from these samples are unsuitable for a performance conclusion or even a stable draw-count comparison. Repeat with a real GPU, a warmed scene, stable asset loading, sufficient frames, matching camera and both the near model and corresponding impostor before claiming any speedup.

### Branch integration and real-GPU sample

In the temporary integration, the branch replaced the `CommonTree_1` slot with `Cincis_Oak_1` in the near, far-bank and background arrays without changing placement order, seed, density or family count. Its authored 6-unit collider height and 0.4-unit radius were independent of the padded impostor quad. That integration and its runtime asset test were removed; the present branch retains the original runtime trees. LOD blending, real-world collision behavior and broader views must be inspected on a new candidate before integration.

The performance fixture was built separately for baseline and candidate and served with browser request interception at the canonical origin, with WebSockets and external requests blocked. On an Apple M4 Pro GPU using Chrome's ANGLE Metal renderer, one warmed 600-frame capture per build used the same north view, 1 cabin, 1000×700 browser viewport, reported 1707×960 canvas and DPR 1.5. Baseline averaged 532 draws and 2,028,986 triangles across passes; the oak branch averaged 534 draws and 1,675,958 triangles. Both were capped at about 60 fps (p95 16.8 vs 16.9 ms), so these samples do not demonstrate a speedup or characterize GPU headroom. Resource/heap counts vary and are not VRAM measurement. JSON and screenshots are saved as `gpu-original` and `gpu-oak`; the fixture uses the branch's model and matching impostor. Captures show the oak as a darker, more detailed broadleaf among the existing stylized trees; further close-range and LOD-switch inspection is necessary.

An additional temporary, uncommitted camera fixture targeted an actual oak instance in the built scene at requested near and far offsets. The close view shows trunk and foliage under game lighting, but other trees obscure the crown; the far view is partly hidden by terrain and does not isolate the same tree. The first far attempt was invalid because orbit controls clamped camera distance; the second raised the test-only limit and recorded an actual camera approximately 165 units away. `oak-game-close-fixture.png` and `oak-game-far-fixture.png` record these views. They do **not** prove an acceptable LOD transition; an unobstructed, same-tree transition or camera-distance sweep is still needed. The test-only camera code was restored immediately after the fixture build and is not included in the source change.

## Quality reassessment

The direct matched-camera image `oak-before-after-comparison.png` shows the prior forest on the left and the first oak candidate on the right. The candidate introduces a wider silhouette and more bark detail, but its clumped card foliage, dark photorealistic texture and regular forked branches are not a convincing visual improvement over the current game trees. The user challenged the quality and that assessment is accepted. The candidate was integrated into the draft too early. Its runtime replacement was removed from the branch; the model, references and measurements remain as research for a redesigned tree. The LOD brightness and diagonal narrowing investigation should be revisited against the redesigned model, not optimized as an acceptance of v2.

An interactive Blender inspection after Computer Use was restored compared the original source and v2 in the same scene. The original has broad, simplified green foliage shapes and warm painted bark; v2 has much finer, photographic leaf and bark detail, visible repeated foliage streaks, and a more regular fork. The perspective view is useful for diagnosing style and construction, but does not match the trees' scale and camera closely enough to rank their silhouettes. The next design should establish a broad, varied oak crown and a simpler painted color treatment in the actual mesh, then check it at equal scale and in game. Merely recoloring the v2 texture would not fix its repetition and branch layout.

## Sapling exploration

Blender 4.1.1's bundled Sapling Tree Gen was run headlessly from a temporary official, SHA-256 verified Blender image; `scripts/explore-cincis-sapling.py` records the parameters and generates an editable local `.blend` plus `cincis-oak-sapling-experiment.glb`. The generated, packed foliage source and prompt are saved here. `oak-sapling-front.png` and `oak-sapling-side.png` are studio captures of the actual export. Its branching has more variation than the handmade v2, but repeated large foliage cards create sharp streaks and fail the visual bar; this is an experiment, not a replacement. File size and alpha overdraw are also unoptimized. The Sapling add-on and the Blender installation are external tools, not bundled runtime assets. Continue by testing a different foliage construction and assessing the resulting actual export against the existing game tree.

## Foliage construction comparison

The Blender viewport exposed why the spray texture failed: each small leaf plane carried a whole branching sprig, creating repeated, needle-like streaks. A single-leaf test removed that repetition but left a sparse, striped crown. A denser five-leaf cluster test produced a fuller crown, but still reads too conifer-like and has flat, spiky edges and a bare trunk. `oak-foliage-tests.png` compares the actual exports; neither is approved. The denser test is reproducible using `scripts/explore-cincis-cluster.py` and the packed `oak-leaf-cluster.png`; `cincis-oak-cluster-v2.glb` remains a non-runtime experiment. It has 6,258 triangles, two materials and 1,585 masked quads; its full-resolution unoptimized texture is about 1.34 MB. It has not passed a GPU-overdraw or in-game visual comparison, and its appearance does not yet meet the user's quality bar.

## Layered canopy test

A later experiment combined a Sapling trunk with 18 large intersecting, generated foliage cards. Its exported geometry had only 676 triangles, but the actual render `oak-layered-canopy-rejected.png` shows vertical card streaks, obvious flatness and exposed branch ends. The unoptimized source texture alone was 2.26 MB. It is rejected: low polygon count and an attractive generated texture did not produce an acceptable 3D tree. No runtime asset or placement changed. The next iteration needs a different construction or better art direction rather than simply increasing or reducing the number of crossed cards.

## Local image-to-3D test

An offline reconstruction test used the MIT-licensed [TripoSR code](https://github.com/VAST-AI-Research/TripoSR) at commit `107cefdc244c39106fa830359024f6a2f1c78871` and [official model](https://huggingface.co/stabilityai/TripoSR), whose `model.ckpt` SHA-256 was `429e2c6b22a0923967459de24d67f05962b235f79cde6b032aa7ed2ffcd970ee`. The generated `oak-reconstruction-reference.png` and edited transparent `oak-reconstruction-cutout.png` supplied the image; the latter was composited on neutral 50% gray and resized to 512×512 for the model. No image was uploaded to a reconstruction service. On Apple MPS, a 96³ extraction with 4,096 query chunks produced 17,773 vertices and 35,850 faces in a short local test. For the experiment, the CPU skimage marching-cubes implementation replaced torchmcubes and its coordinate ordering was adapted; the saved `oak-triposr-rejected.glb` was rotated upright and normals repaired for inspection.

The actual `oak-triposr-front.png` and `oak-triposr-side.png` renders show a dark, faceted, lumpy result with missing fine branches and poor foliage. It is unsuitable in both appearance and raw geometry cost and has not been integrated. This is one low-resolution single-image test with a locally modified extraction path, not a general evaluation of TripoSR. The `.glb` and screenshots remain only to document the failure. The temporary model weights and Python environment were removed after the test.

## Existing foliage construction and density test

Inspection of the source `CommonTree_1.gltf` found 1,920 triangles in its foliage material: 960 disconnected two-triangle planes. Its foliage image has a mostly flat green color with a mask containing multiple separated leaf silhouettes; the remaining reported triangles are in the bark material. This is source inspection, not a measurement of runtime overdraw. The old tree was used only for study, not as geometry or texture in the experiments.

A test using 540 smaller planes and the generated painted oak cluster produced the fuller but dark, streaked crown in `oak-dense-cards-front.png`. A second used the same card arrangement and an independently drawn flat oak-leaf mask; `oak-flat-cards-front.png` shows a thin, fragmented crown. Both retain the earlier regular-forked trunk. Neither is an improvement ready for integration. The experiments show that simply increasing card count or flattening texture colors does not by itself fix silhouette, card orientation, crown density, lighting and branch design. Revisit those together and measure masked overdraw before treating the method as an optimization.

## Beech style study

The local context above suggests beech in higher oak–beech forests; it does not establish a shoreline inventory. [Kew](https://www.kew.org/plants/european-beech) and the [Woodland Trust](https://www.woodlandtrust.org.uk/trees-woods-and-wildlife/british-trees/a-z-of-british-trees/common-beech/) describe smooth gray bark and pointed oval leaves with wavy edges; the canopy varies between open and forest growth. `beech-foliage-generated.png` is a separate generated beech leaf texture based on the style lineup, not oak leaves. `scripts/build-cincis-beech.mjs` creates `cincis-beech-prototype.glb`, whose studio front and side renders are `beech-prototype-front.png` and `beech-prototype-side.png`.

The first model and a denser revision both show pointed foliage-card streaks rather than the full beech canopy in the concept. The gray trunk reads as a plain cylinder and the branch junctions are crude compared with the oak. This is a work-in-progress study, not a finished tree or a runtime replacement. The initial export was 204,944 bytes with 1,240 wood and 1,728 foliage triangles. Those counts do not measure masked overdraw, shadows or frame time. Inspect the trunk texture, branch transitions and canopy in Blender, then compare a revised export under the same camera and game lighting as the oak before integration.


The beech was subsequently imported into the interactive Blender comparison scene, although it overlapped the oak and an isolated side-by-side composition was not completed. A generated smooth-gray bark map was then applied to the beech; the current export is 217,976 bytes, with the same triangle counts. The updated front and side renders show a less featureless trunk, but the branch intersections and dense spiky crown remain unresolved. The bark’s horizontal markings may read as birch at close range and require further species review; the texture’s requested seamlessness has not been verified. The current model remains a prototype outside the runtime.


A follow-up changed only the foliage source and card density: `beech-leaf-spray-generated.png` has five distinct oval leaves without the large twigs baked into the earlier spray. `scripts/explore-beech-spray.mjs` produces `cincis-beech-spray-study.glb`; `beech-spray-front.png` and `beech-spray-side.png` show the actual export. In these studio views the crown reads more clearly as broad leaves and has fewer long twig-shaped streaks, while retaining sharp card edges, repeated leaf groupings, and an unfinished trunk and branch joins. This is a promising isolated change, not a verified game-ready model. The same geometry with blended radial foliage normals was also tested but did not resolve the silhouette, so it was not adopted. The new 238,744-byte export has 360 foliage cards versus 216 in the previous version; test masked-pixel cost and real GPU behavior before drawing any performance conclusion.


## Branch-junction remesh study

The separated-leaf beech had visible overlapping tube intersections. A voxel-remesh test of the original tubes initially lost most of the trunk: their ends and seams were open. `scripts/explore-beech-watertight.mjs` generates closed ring-and-cap tubes, and the version of `scripts/explore-beech-remesh.py` in commit `48045fc` used Blender 4.1.1 to voxel-remesh, smooth, decimate and unwrap the wood. The current script instead builds the coarse no-decimation variant described below. Run the generator with Node 24 and an explicit intermediate output path, then pass that path and an output GLB after `--` to Blender with the Python script. The saved `cincis-beech-remesh-study.glb` and its front and side studio renders show a continuous trunk and softer branch joins; bark mapping is uneven and branch anatomy is still simplistic.

The 266,868-byte export contains 2,880 foliage triangles and 1,787 wood triangles, versus 1,240 wood triangles in the earlier beech. This is not a runtime performance measurement. Blender warned that the remeshed wood mesh may export incorrectly; it appeared in the studio renderer but needs validity, normals, UV seams, texture and actual-game checks before use. This study does not change the runtime.


A follow-up isolated the previous export warning: the decimation pass created duplicate faces; validation removed them but left small holes. A coarser 0.10 voxel remesh without decimation avoids that warning and re-imports with no non-manifold trunk edges after welding split export vertices. `cincis-beech-remesh-coarse-study.glb` and `beech-remesh-coarse-front.png` / `beech-remesh-coarse-side.png` show that variant with cylindrical bark projection. Its 299,940-byte GLB has 2,880 foliage triangles and 4,440 wood triangles, including 12 zero-area wood triangles; Blender re-import drops some degenerate faces. Removing the degenerates with a separate cleanup produced four boundary edges in a re-import test, so neither variant is yet a validated runtime mesh. This higher geometry count, the blunted branch ends and bark texture need further work before acceptance. The remaining foliage texture sampler warning also needs review.

## Oak lighting experiments
